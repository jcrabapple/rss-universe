Web & hosted readers

Nothing to install — read in the browser, sync everywhere.

SaaSfree tier

The biggest hosted reader. Clean, fast, and AI-assisted (Leo) for monitoring and research. Free up to 100 sources.

feedly.com

SaaSfree tier

The power-user pick: rules, keyword alerts, global full-text search, and newsletter-to-RSS. Free tier supports up to 150 sources.

inoreader.com

open source

Hosted reader with social layers: train your feed with a learning filter, share stories, and read the original site view.

newsblur.com

open sourcepaid

A fast, calm, well-designed paid reader ($5/mo). Bonus: it mints an email address that turns newsletters into RSS.

feedbin.com

BazQux

paid

Quiet, fast, reliable hosted reader with RSS/Atom/JSON feed support, comments, and full-text extraction.

bazqux.com

free tier

Google Reader's spiritual heir: folders, star, share, and a social following model without the algorithm.

theoldreader.com

Birchfeed

SaaSpaid

New sync service (Aug 2026) that speaks Google Reader, FreshRSS and Fever, so the app you already use just keeps working — plus a keyboard-first web reader, automations, and newsletter-to-RSS. 14-day trial, then $29–39/yr.

birchfeed.app

Readwise Reader

SaaSpaid

Read-later and RSS in one app, with serious highlighting, search, and AI note-taking on top of your feeds.

readwise.io/read

Folo

paid

AI-native reader from the RSSNext crew, built to pair with RSSHub. Roughly $1/mo — an "info aquarium" of one stream.

folo.is

Glanced

SaaSfree tier

Newer cross-platform reader (web, desktop, iOS, Android) that pulls feeds, newsletters and podcasts into one library, with AI summaries and duplicate-story merging.

glanced.io

CommaFeed

open sourcefree tier

Lightweight Google Reader-style reader — use the hosted instance or run it yourself with Docker.

commafeed.com

Syndicator

free

Ranked daily-sweep reader: groups repeated stories, learns from what you open, and installs as a PWA. Your public profile doubles as an RSS linklog.

syndicator.one

SereneReader

SaaSfree tier

Calm, chronologically sorted reader that leaves things out on purpose: no ads, no AI summaries, no algorithmic "relevance", no tracking pixels. Focus mode gives one article the whole screen, j/k keyboard nav, OPML in and out; free covers 150 feeds, Pro ($5/mo) adds 250 feeds, more history and the API.

serenereader.com

FeedCity

SaaSpaid

Single-column reader with no unread count: filter the stream by text, audio or video, group feeds into lists that publish their own RSS, Atom and OPML feeds, and bookmark anything with a bookmarklet or iOS shortcut. feed.city

Feeddi

SaaSfree

Hosted Google Reader-style reader: chronological, folders, unread counts, j/k/s/m shortcuts, muted words, OPML in and out, and Bluesky profiles as feeds. Free up to 2,000 feeds, with Android and Huawei apps, Chrome, Firefox and Edge extensions, and 21 interface languages.

feeddi.com

Self-hosted readers

Your feeds, your server, your data.

Miniflux

open sourcefree

The minimalist's dream: a single Go binary + PostgreSQL, text-first UI, Fever and Google Reader APIs for mobile clients. Sips ~100MB RAM.

miniflux.app

ReactFlux

open sourcefree

A polished React front-end for Miniflux, deployable as static files or Docker — the modern UI Miniflux itself skips.

github.com/electh/ReactFlux

FreshRSS

open sourcefree

The most popular PHP self-hosted reader: categories, tags, filters, stats, webhooks, extensions, and multi-protocol APIs.

freshrss.org

simple feed reader

open sourcefree

Multi-user self-hosted reader (Symfony API + Angular SPA) with full-text article extraction, email digests, saved searches and OPML. One-line Docker install.

github.com/larspohlmann/simple-feed-reader

Tiny Tiny RSS

open sourcefree

The veteran PHP reader with a plugin system and dedicated mobile clients. A settled workflow for seasoned self-hosters.

tt-rss.org

Yarr

open sourcefree

"Yet another rss reader": one binary, embedded SQLite, zero fuss. Runs as a tray app or headless over the browser. Fever API included.

github.com/nkanaev/yarr

Fusion

open sourcefree

Yarr's younger, prettier sibling — Go + TypeScript, proper PWA, Fever API. Deliberately ships zero AI features.

github.com/0x2e/fusion

FeedFerret

open sourcefree

New 2026 multi-user reader (Docker, Postgres or SQLite) with rule engines, webhooks, ntfy/Telegram alerts, and a Google Reader API for mobile clients.

github.com/moby3012/feedferret

Readfine

open sourcefree

Self-hosted Python + HTMX reader: CSS-selector scraping for pages without feeds, rule-based filtering, full-text extraction, and bring-your-own-key AI summaries and morning briefings.

github.com/jakublibik/readfine

FriRSS

open sourcefree

A modern self-hosted web front-end for FreshRSS: three-column desktop layout, installable PWA with offline reading, Readability extraction, and nested labels — over the Google Reader API.

github.com/Fripix/frirss

Selfoss

open sourcefree

A "multipurpose" PHP reader: RSS, live streams, and mashups in one self-hosted web app.

selfoss.aditu.de

Leed

open sourcefree

Minimalist PHP/MySQL aggregator: a cron job fetches and parses every feed in the background, so pages render instantly instead of waiting on the network. Responsive down to a phone, OPML in and out, AGPL-licensed and still shipping releases after 13 years.

github.com/LeedRSS/Leed

Nextcloud News

open sourcefree

The news reader that comes with Nextcloud: subscribe in the browser, read in the web UI or the Android app, with categories, starred items and automatic cleanup. Installs in one click from the app store, and a self-hosted Nextcloud turns into a full reader stack with your own accounts.

apps.nextcloud.com/apps/news

Salut

open sourcefree

A self-hosted start page rather than a reader: YAML-configured cards that pull RSS alongside calendar, GitHub, weather and search. No login by design (it is meant for your own network), English and French, systemd installer included.

github.com/plbrault/Salut

Huginn

Self-hosted automation agents that can watch, scrape, transform, and emit RSS feeds — your own private IFTTT.

github.com/huginn/huginn

feed-repeat

open sourcefree

Haskell tool that re-publishes older entries from source feeds into new ones, using weighted sampling so stale posts come back more often and recent ones less. Caches sources, filters by minimum age, handles RSS, Atom and RDF, and ships static binaries, a Docker image and a NixOS module.

github.com/abhin4v/feed-repeat

feed-mixer

open sourcefree

Reads an OPML list, fetches every feed concurrently and writes one combined Atom feed — built to run on a GitHub Actions schedule and publish to GitHub Pages, so no server of your own is needed. Keeps a disk cache so one flaky feed does not drop out of the mix.

github.com/IndieWebClubBlr/feed-mixer

open sourcefree

Turns your Mastodon bookmarks into an RSS feed your reader can subscribe to. Stateless: it stores nothing, and the credentials ride inside the secret feed URL. MIT-licensed, so anyone can run their own copy; the hosted instance is free.

bookmark-rss.woodland.cafe

byedoom.com

Standards & formats

The boring stuff that makes everything else work.

RSS 2.0 Specification

spec

The canonical RSS 2.0 spec, maintained by the RSS Advisory Board.

rssboard.org/rss-specification

RSS Advisory Board

org

The org that maintains RSS 2.0, plus a feed validator and assorted tools.

rssboard.org

W3C Feed Validator

tool

Validate your RSS and Atom feeds before you ship them to the world.

validator.w3.org/feed

JSON Feed

spec

The JSON syndication format (v1.1) from Manton Reece and Brent Simmons — same idea as RSS, no XML.

jsonfeed.org

JSON Feed Validator

tool

Check a JSON Feed document against the spec.

validator.jsonfeed.org

OPML

spec

The outline format every reader uses to import/export subscriptions. Your feeds, portable.

opml.org

WebSub

spec

The W3C standard for real-time feed push: hubs ping subscribers the instant something publishes.

w3.org/TR/websub

Atom (RFC 4287)

spec

The Atom Syndication Format — the more rigorously specified cousin of RSS.

datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc4287

Podcasting 2.0

spec

An open RSS namespace extending feeds for transcripts, chapters, funding, and more — backwards compatible.

podcasting2.org

Podcast Index

open sourcefree

The open, free podcast feed index and search API — podcasting without the platform.

podcastindex.org

Podcast namespace repo

open source

The <podcast:> namespace spec, released to the public domain (CC0).

github.com/Podcastindex-org/podcast-namespace

Mastodon built-in feeds

built in

Every Mastodon account and tag page already publishes an RSS feed, auto-discoverable from the page: add .rss to a profile URL (dmv.community/@name.rss) or a tag URL (dmv.community/tags/rss.rss) and any reader can follow it. No account, token or third-party service needed.

docs.joinmastodon.org/user/network

Mastodon Bookmark RSS

open sourcefree

Turns your Mastodon bookmarks into an RSS feed your reader can subscribe to. Stateless: it stores nothing, and the credentials ride inside the secret feed URL. MIT-licensed, so anyone can run their own copy; the hosted instance is free.

bookmark-rss.woodland.cafe

Bridgy Fed

open sourcefree

Bridge between the fediverse, the open web and Bluesky: a personal site with a feed shows up as a followable fediverse account, and bridged accounts get timeline feeds in Atom and other formats. CC0-licensed code, run as a free service.

fed.brid.gy

MastoFeed

SaaSfree

Hosted pipe from RSS or Atom straight into a Mastodon account via OAuth: connect, add a feed, every new item posts itself.

mastofeed.org

RSS Parrot

SaaSfree

Reverse direction: mention @birb@rss-parrot.net with any site, Substack, podcast or Bluesky profile and it spawns a Mastodon account that toots each new post.

rss-parrot.net

FeedEcho

open sourcefree

Self-hosted cross-poster: watches RSS, Atom or JSON feeds and echoes new items to Mastodon, Bluesky, micro.blog or email — Jinja2 templates, keyword filters, drip pacing, Docker.

github.com/jcrabapple/feedecho

FeedEcho Hosted

SaaSpaid

The same engine as a managed service at feedecho.net — invite-only beta, 14-day free trial. Feeds post to social or inbox on autopilot, with alt-text generation and digest mode.

feedecho.net

Micro.blog

SaaSfree tier

POSSE platform: post at your own domain and it syndicates everywhere — Mastodon, Bluesky, Threads, Tumblr, Nostr and more — with ActivityPub and an RSS feed for every post. Also serves photo blogs, newsletters and native apps, and bundles the Inkwell feed reader. $5/month to host your domain; free account if you blog elsewhere.

micro.blog

Reeder

paid

The design benchmark on Mac/iOS. The new timeline-style app mixes RSS with podcasts, YouTube, Mastodon and Bluesky in one stream.

reederapp.com

Tapestry

freemium

The Iconfactory's universal Apple app: a unified chronological timeline fed by connectors for RSS, Atom, JSON Feed, Mastodon, Bluesky, Tumblr, YouTube, Reddit and podcasts — plus third-party connectors, muffle/mute rules, and iCloud position sync. Free; Premium is $1.99/mo, $19.99/yr or $79.99 lifetime.

tapestry.iconfactory.com

News Explorer

paid

Dutch-developed Apple reader covering every platform — iPhone, iPad, Mac, Watch and TV — synced over iCloud with no account to sign up for. Pulls RSS, Atom, JSON, Mastodon, Reddit, YouTube and podcasts, then does full-text extraction, article comments, smart filters, prefilters and rich notes. $4.99 on iOS, $9.99 on macOS, no subscription.

betamagic.nl/products/newsexplorer.html

Fedi.Tips: RSS on Mastodon

free guide

Plain-language walkthrough for following Mastodon accounts and hashtags from any feed reader (add .rss to the address), following RSS feeds from Mastodon via RSS Parrot, and subscribing to PeerTube channels. Public posts only: replies and non-public posts do not appear in the feeds.

fedi.tips

Friendica

open sourcefree

Fediverse platform that treats RSS as a first-class citizen: follow any RSS or Atom feed (blogs, WordPress, arbitrary pages with a discoverable feed) and its items land in your timeline as normal posts. AGPL-licensed and self-hostable.

friendi.ca

Switching readers? It's painless.

Every serious reader imports and exports OPML — the one file format that carries all your subscriptions. Export from your current app, import into the next, done in under a minute. Don't let sunk cost keep you in a reader you don't like.
